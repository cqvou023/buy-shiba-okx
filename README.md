### 5. Choose market, limit, or convert

For a straightforward purchase, you will usually encounter one of three approaches.

#### Market order

A market order executes against available orders in the order book. It is designed for speed, but the final average price may differ from the price you first saw, especially in a thin or rapidly moving market.

Market orders can be convenient for a small purchase when immediate execution matters more than specifying an exact price. They are less suitable when you want strict control over the maximum price paid.

#### Limit order

A limit order lets you specify the price at which you are willing to buy SHIB. The order will execute only if the market reaches that price and sufficient sellers are available.

The tradeoff is simple:

- You get more control over price.
- The order may remain open.
- The order may fill only partially.
- The order may never execute.

A limit order is useful when you have a defined entry price and are willing to wait.

#### Convert

Convert is generally easier for users who do not want to work directly with an order book. You select the asset you are paying with, select SHIB as the asset you want to receive, enter the amount, and review the quote.

The final conversion rate can include a spread or service cost that is different from the spot trading fee. Always compare the quoted amount received with the amount you would receive through spot trading after fees.
